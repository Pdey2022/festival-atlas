Explore every celebration

Indian festival guides

Browse 62 guides to India’s living traditions. Seasons are typical timing; follow each guide’s calendar link for sourced annual listings.

Bastar Dussehra

August–October · Central

A long cycle of rituals honours Goddess Danteshwari, bringing together local communities, ceremonial chariots and regional deities.

Bathukamma

September–October · South

Women arrange seasonal flowers in tiered stacks and gather around them to sing and celebrate.

Bhagoria

February–March · Central

Bhil and Bhilala communities gather at lively fairs before Holi, with music, dancing and market stalls.

Bohag Bihu

April · Northeast

Also called Rongali Bihu, this spring celebration welcomes the Assamese New Year with Bihu dance, music and festive meals.

Bonalu

July–August · South

Devotees offer decorated pots of food to the goddess Mahakali, accompanied by local processions.

Carnival

February–March · West

Colourful floats, music and dancing fill the streets in the days leading up to Lent.

Chapchar Kut

March · Northeast

A Mizo spring festival with music, traditional dress and dances including the bamboo dance, Cheraw.

Chhath Puja

October–November · East, North

Devotees offer prayers to the setting and rising sun at rivers and ponds, with fasting and carefully prepared offerings.

Dev Deepawali

November; sometimes October · North

On Kartik Purnima, thousands of lamps illuminate the Ganges ghats in a spectacular riverside celebration.

Diwali

October–November · North, West, South

Diyas, family gatherings, sweets and worship mark the festival of lights in the capital.

Dree

July · Northeast

The Apatani community gathers for agricultural rituals, songs and dances, offering prayers for a healthy crop.

Durga Puja

September–October · East, Northeast

Neighbourhoods become open-air galleries of elaborate pandals and Durga idols. Dhak drums, dhunuchi dance and shared bhog bring people together in a celebration of creativity and devotion.

Eid al-Fitr

Moves through the solar year · North, Islands

Prayers, charitable giving and festive meals mark the end of Ramadan; dates depend on the lunar calendar.

Ganesh Chaturthi

August–September · West, South

Ganesha idols are welcomed into homes and public pandals, with prayers, modak sweets and immersion processions.

Gangaur

March–April · West

Women honour Gauri with rituals, decorated images and colourful processions.

Garia Puja

April · Northeast

Traditional prayers and community celebrations seek wellbeing and a prosperous agricultural season.

Gudi Padwa

March–April · West

Families raise decorated gudis outside their homes to welcome the new year.

Harela

July · North

Seedlings grown in the days before the festival symbolise greenery, prosperity and the agricultural season.

Hareli

July–August · Central

Farming communities honour agricultural tools and celebrate the green season with local games and traditions.

Hemis Festival

June–July · North

Masked cham dances and monastery rituals commemorate Guru Padmasambhava.

Herath

February–March · North

The Kashmiri observance of Shivratri centres on family worship, ritual offerings and festive food.

Holi

February–March · North, West, East, Central

The Braj region’s distinctive celebrations combine devotional singing and colourful traditions over several days.

Hornbill Festival

December · Northeast

Naga communities share performances, crafts, food and traditions at a major annual cultural gathering.

Island Tourism Festival

Schedule varies · Islands

A public cultural programme showcases island communities through performances, exhibitions and local food; confirm the organiser’s current schedule.

Kali Puja

October–November · East

Homes and temples honour Goddess Kali with lamps, offerings and evening worship around the new moon.

Karam

August–September · East

Community celebrations centre on the Karam tree, with songs and dances expressing hopes for wellbeing and prosperity.

Khajuraho Dance Festival

February · Central

Indian classical dance traditions are showcased against the backdrop of Khajuraho’s temple heritage.

Kharchi Puja

June–July · Northeast

The Fourteen Gods are honoured through rituals and a public fair at the Chaturdasha Devata temple.

Kullu Dussehra

September–October · North

Valley deities are brought to Kullu in a gathering centred on Lord Raghunath, with processions and cultural programmes.

Lohri

January · North

Families and communities gather around bonfires with folk songs and seasonal foods.

Losar

February–March · Northeast

Monpa communities welcome the new year with monastery prayers, family visits and traditional food.

Losoong / Namsoong

December–January · Northeast

Bhutia and Lepcha communities mark seasonal renewal through their distinct traditions and celebrations.

Magh Bihu

January · Northeast

Community feasts and ceremonial meji bonfires celebrate the end of the harvest season.

Mandi Shivratri

February–March · North

Local deities, devotees and performers gather for a celebration associated with Maha Shivratri.

Mim Kut

August–September · Northeast

A traditional maize-season observance combines remembrance of departed relatives with community customs.

Moatsu

May · Northeast

After sowing, Ao communities celebrate with songs, dances and communal gatherings.

Mysuru Dasara

September–October · South

An illuminated palace, performances and the celebrated procession make Dasara a signature Mysuru experience.

Nariyal Purnima

August · West

Fishing communities offer coconuts to the sea, accompanied by prayers and local celebrations.

Navratri

September–October · West

Nine nights of devotion to the goddess are celebrated with circular garba dancing and dandiya raas.

Nongkrem Dance Festival

Autumn; dates vary · Northeast

Khasi ceremonial traditions and dances express thanksgiving and prayers for prosperity.

Onam

August–September · South

Floral pookalam designs, the onasadya feast and cultural performances celebrate the homecoming of King Mahabali.

Phool Dei

March · North

Children place flowers at doorsteps to welcome spring and offer blessings for the household.

Poila Boishakh

April · East

New account books, cultural programmes and generous Bengali meals mark the start of the Bengali year.

Pongal

January · South

Families prepare freshly harvested rice in a ceremonial pot and honour the sun and cattle over several days.

Pushkar Fair

October–November · West

Around Kartik Purnima, pilgrims and visitors gather for a traditional fair, cultural events and lakeside rituals.

Raja Parba

June · East

A celebration associated with the earth’s fertility, featuring swings, pitha and a pause in agricultural work.

Rath Yatra

June–July · East

The deities Jagannath, Balabhadra and Subhadra travel in monumental wooden chariots through Puri.

Rose Festival

February–March · North

Flower displays and cultural activities celebrate the city’s garden heritage.

Sangai Festival

November · Northeast

A tourism festival presents the state’s dance, crafts, food and cultural traditions.

Sankranti

January · South

Rangoli, decorated cattle and family gatherings mark the harvest season.

Sarhul

March–April · East

Several Adivasi communities honour sacred groves and the flowering sal tree with rituals, music and dancing.

Shigmo

March · West

Folk dances, processions and elaborate floats bring Goan spring traditions into public celebrations.

Sonepur Mela

November–December · East

Around Kartik Purnima, pilgrims, performers and traders gather at this historic riverside fair.

Surajkund International Crafts Mela

February · North

Artisans and performers gather for a major cultural fair showcasing handicrafts, folk traditions and regional food.

Tamil Puthandu

April · South

Temple visits, festive meals and decorated entrances welcome the Tamil New Year.

Teej

July–August · North

Songs, swings and gatherings celebrate the arrival of the monsoon and the traditions of Teej.

Ugadi

March–April · South

Homes are decorated with mango leaves and families share ugadi pachadi, whose contrasting flavours symbolise the experiences of life.

Uttarayan

January · West

Rooftops fill with kite flyers during Makar Sankranti, with festive food and colourful skies.

Vaisakhi

April · North

Harvest celebrations coincide with the commemoration of the formation of the Khalsa, with gurdwara visits and community meals.

Vishu

April · South

The day begins with viewing the auspicious Vishukkani arrangement, followed by gifts and family meals.

Wangala

October–November · Northeast

Garo communities celebrate the harvest with drumming and dancing, giving thanks to the sun deity.

Yaoshang

February–March · Northeast

A multiday spring celebration combines devotional traditions, local sports and thabal chongba dancing.
