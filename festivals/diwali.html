Diwali

दीपावली

Diyas, family gatherings, sweets and worship mark the festival of lights in the capital.

View calendar

The story

Diwali, or Deepavali, is a festival of lights celebrated through many regional and community traditions. Lamps, decorated homes, sweets and family gatherings are familiar features, but the stories and ceremonies are not identical everywhere. Worship of Lakshmi is important in many Hindu households, while other communities give the season their own meanings. Public illuminations and markets offer visitors a way to experience the atmosphere without needing access to a private home. Choose a destination and local programme first: the surrounding festive days matter as much as the main night.

What you’ll experience

  • Lighting diyas and decorating homes or public spaces
  • Family worship and gatherings according to local tradition
  • Exchanging sweets, greetings and gifts

Food & festive dress

Mithai and savoury snacks are widely shared; recipes differ across India. Nuts, milk and ghee are common, so ask about ingredients.

Festive clothing is common. Keep loose fabrics away from oil lamps and choose practical footwear for crowded markets.

  • Confirm local public events and transport arrangements before choosing a viewing route.
  • Accept household invitations respectfully and ask before photographing a family’s worship.
