Devotion · Community

Eid al-Fitr

Prayers, charitable giving and festive meals mark the end of Ramadan; dates depend on the lunar calendar.

Plan your stayView calendar

The story

Eid al-Fitr marks the completion of Ramadan, bringing Muslim communities together in prayer, generosity and celebration. Family visits, greetings and shared meals are central to many households, alongside care for people in need. The occasion is observed across India, with distinct local food and cultural traditions rather than a single standard celebration. Its date follows the Islamic calendar and local moon-sighting announcements, so a forecast date should not be presented as confirmed. In Delhi, the neighbourhoods around major mosques can be lively, but worship spaces remain places of prayer.

What you’ll experience

  • Congregational Eid prayer
  • Charitable giving and support for others
  • Family visits, greetings and shared festive meals

Food & festive dress

Sweet vermicelli preparations and other festive foods vary by region and family. Check ingredients and opening times with individual restaurants.

Worshippers often wear their best or new clothes. Visitors entering a mosque should follow its requirements for modest dress and footwear.
