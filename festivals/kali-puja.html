Kali Puja

কালীপূজা

Homes and temples honour Goddess Kali with lamps, offerings and evening worship around the new moon.

View calendar

The story

Kali Puja honours the goddess Kali and is particularly prominent in West Bengal. Temples, household shrines and neighbourhood pandals each create their own setting for worship, often with elaborate images and lighting. The observance falls around the autumn new moon associated with Diwali, but its devotional focus is distinct. Kolkata offers both major temple traditions and community celebrations; a carefully chosen neighbourhood route is more practical than trying to see everything in one night. Ritual timing and visitor access should be checked with each venue.

What you’ll experience

  • Worship of Kali at temples and household or community shrines
  • Decorated pandals and goddess images
  • Evening lighting and neighbourhood gatherings

Food & festive dress

Prasad and meals vary widely by temple and community. Ask organisers what is available and avoid assuming that every venue serves the same offerings.

Festive clothing is common, while visitors should choose respectful dress and comfortable shoes for queues and walking.

Visiting respectfully

  • Check whether photography is permitted inside a temple or pandal.
  • Keep routes short enough for your group, and arrange a meeting point away from the busiest entrance.
Accessibility & practical planning

Step-free routes, toilets, seating, quiet spaces and crowd controls have not been audited. Ask the venue about your specific needs and check transport arrangements before visiting.
