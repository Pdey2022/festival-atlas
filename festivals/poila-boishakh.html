Poila Boishakh

পয়লা বৈশাখ

New account books, cultural programmes and generous Bengali meals mark the start of the Bengali year.

View calendar

The story

Poila Boishakh marks the beginning of the Bengali calendar year. In West Bengal, it brings together family meals, new clothes, greetings and cultural programmes. Some businesses observe Halkhata, the symbolic opening of a new accounts ledger, often welcoming customers with sweets. Music, neighbourhood activity and household traditions give the occasion a character that extends beyond a single religious setting. Kolkata is a useful base, though celebrations also take place across Bengali communities. Follow the local calendar rather than assuming every country or community observes an identical date.

What you’ll experience

  • New Year greetings and family gatherings
  • Halkhata customs among participating businesses
  • Music, cultural programmes and household alpana designs

Food & festive dress

Bengali sweets and festive meals are prominent, but menus vary by household and restaurant. Book a special restaurant meal directly if that is your preferred experience.

Saris, kurtas and other new festive clothing are common choices; there is no required visitor costume.

Visiting respectfully

  • Choose an announced cultural programme for public participation.
  • Ask a shopkeeper before photographing a Halkhata ceremony; it is a business and devotional custom, not a staged attraction.
Accessibility & practical planning

Step-free routes, toilets, seating, quiet spaces and crowd controls have not been audited. Ask the venue about your specific needs and check transport arrangements before visiting.
